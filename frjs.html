Portable FRJS

CAD & fabricationSubsystem integrationExperimental testing

Project Overview

I designed and developed a handheld Focused Rotary Jet Spinning (FRJS) system to miniaturize a traditionally bench-scale fiber manufacturing process. Conducted through undergraduate research in the H.B. Chang Lab, the project combined mechanical design, compact packaging, subsystem integration, and experimental testing.

I personally completed the CAD design, fabrication, assembly, and testing of the prototype. The system successfully produced fibers in a controlled environment and achieved 95% collection efficiency by volume during enclosed testing.

From Bench-Scale Process to Handheld Device

The design brought together three interacting functions: high-speed spinneret rotation, focused airflow, and controlled solution delivery. Miniaturizing these functions required considering how the components would fit together while preserving the operation of the fiber-production process.

My design work focused on integrating these subsystems into a handheld assembly while addressing rotational stability, airflow performance, and access to the solution-delivery hardware.

Spinneret Drive and Mechanical Integration

I integrated a battery-powered pencil grinder to drive the spinneret. This provided the rotating element of the device and shaped the mechanical layout of the handheld assembly.

I developed CAD models to package the drive, spinneret, and supporting components together. The design required coordinating the rotating assembly with the surrounding airflow and solution-delivery interfaces.

Airflow and Solution Delivery

The prototype incorporated a focused air-jet system alongside the rotating spinneret. Integrating the airflow hardware required considering its placement relative to the spinneret and the available space within the compact assembly.

A peristaltic pump mounted on the handheld stand delivered solution to the spinneret. I integrated the pump and delivery path with the mechanical assembly, bringing solution delivery, rotation, and airflow together in one prototype.

Prototyping and Experimental Testing

I used iterative prototyping and testing to evaluate how the assembled subsystems interacted during operation. These observations guided design refinements while balancing rotational stability, airflow performance, and compact packaging.

The prototype successfully produced fibers under controlled conditions. To assess collection performance, we fully enclosed the system and compared the volume deposited on the collection plate with the volume collected elsewhere in the enclosure.

Results and Project Outcome

The enclosed test achieved 95% collection efficiency by volume, based on the volume collected on the plate divided by the total volume recovered from the plate and elsewhere in the enclosure.

This result demonstrated successful fiber production and collection under the tested conditions. The project also gave me experience taking a research device from CAD through fabrication, assembly, and experimental evaluation, using observed performance to guide mechanical and subsystem-integration decisions.
