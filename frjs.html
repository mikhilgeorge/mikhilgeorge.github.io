Project Overview

I designed and prototyped a handheld focused rotary jet spinning system, miniaturizing a bench-scale nanofiber process into a 500 g assembly. My work combined CAD, mechanical fabrication, rotating hardware, solution delivery, and airflow integration.

In comparative enclosed testing, the six-channel air-jet configuration increased fiber collection efficiency from approximately 85% to 95%, based on recovered fiber volume. This subsystem-level result does not establish full operating-condition validation of the integrated handheld device.

From Bench-Scale Process to Handheld Device

Focused Rotary Jet Spinning (FRJS) is a fiber-manufacturing technique used in nanofabrication. Rapid rotation drives a polymer solution out of the spinneret, drawing it into fine fibers, while focused air streams guide the fibers toward a collector. The resulting fibrous structures can support applications such as tissue-engineering scaffolds.

The original bench-scale setup occupied roughly 2 × 2 × 2 ft. Miniaturization required compactly integrating the rotating assembly, focused airflow, and solution feed while accounting for rotor stability and access for assembly and testing.

Mechanical Integration

I modeled and packaged the high-speed drive, spinneret, and support components in SolidWorks, coordinating shaft alignment and rotating mass distribution with the air-jet and solution-delivery interfaces.

Spinneret Redesign

I recessed the spinneret stem into the main body, shifting rotating mass closer to the drive connection to improve stability at approximately 10,000 RPM. The coupling geometry was refined in SolidWorks and evaluated in more than 20 tests.

I also angled the spokes to create more clearance for air from the inner-focused jets to pass through the spinneret. Coordinating the spoke geometry with the air-jet arrangement connected the mechanical redesign with the airflow needed to guide and collect fibers.

Airflow and Solution Delivery

I selected a six-channel air-jet design with three outer-focused jets and three inner-focused jets to improve fiber collection. The two sets of jets were arranged around the spinneret to guide the fiber stream toward the collector. Integrating this arrangement into a compact air-jet ring substantially reduced the size compared with conventional bench-top setups, while preserving the airflow paths needed for the process.

A peristaltic pump mounted on the handheld stand delivered solution to the spinneret at a steady flow rate of approximately 5 mL/min. I integrated the pump and delivery path with the mechanical assembly, bringing solution delivery, rotation, and airflow together in one prototype.

Results and Project Outcome

Comparative enclosed testing measured an increase in fiber collection efficiency from approximately 85% to 95% using the six-channel annular air-jet configuration. Efficiency was evaluated as the fraction of recovered fiber volume deposited on the target collection plate.

The project advanced a portable FRJS prototype through CAD, fabrication, integration, and subsystem testing. Additional integrated-system characterization remains distinct from the measured enclosed-test result.
